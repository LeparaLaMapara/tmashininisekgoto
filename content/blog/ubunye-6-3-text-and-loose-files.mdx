# Ubunye Engine Part 6.3: Text and Loose Files, Made Searchable

*Example 03 of eleven. Runs in three minutes forty.*

---

## The problem

Most of what an organisation knows is not in a table.

It is in reviews customers wrote, contracts somebody signed, policies in a
folder, notes from a call. It is text, written by people, for people. No
columns, no types, no keys.

You cannot query it, and the useful question is never "find me the word refund".
It is "what are people unhappy about", which no amount of searching for words
will answer.

Before any of that is possible, the text has to be turned into something with
edges.

## Two kinds of source, two lines of settings

The reviews are already in a table, so they come in like any table.

The files are not. They are files on a drive, and what you want is their
contents:

```yaml
    files:
      format: binary
      path: "/Volumes/.../corpus"
```

`binary` hands you each file as raw bytes, along with its path, its size and
when it was modified. That is deliberately the lowest possible level, because a
PDF, a photograph and a text file have nothing in common except being a pile of
bytes with a name. What you do with them next is your business.

This one is read only, and the engine tells you so if you try to write with it,
rather than letting you find out later.

---

## The idea worth stopping on: overlap

Once you have the text, it has to be cut into pieces. Pieces small enough that
each one is about a single thing, because a whole contract is not a useful unit
of meaning.

The obvious way is to cut every 500 characters. And the obvious way loses
information at every cut.

Imagine the sentence that matters is *"refunds are not available after thirty
days unless the item was faulty"*. Your cut lands in the middle of it. Now the
first piece ends with "refunds are not available after thirty" and the second
begins with "days unless the item was faulty". Ask later whether faulty items can
be refunded and neither piece can tell you, because the sentence that knew has
been cut in half and both halves are misleading.

The fix is to make the pieces **overlap**. Each chunk starts somewhat before the
previous one ended, so every sentence sits whole inside at least one chunk.

You pay for it in storage, and it is not close. 210 documents became **262
chunks**, so roughly a quarter more pieces than documents, and the ones near a
boundary appear twice. That is the trade: a bit more storage against never
losing a sentence to a cut.

---

## What the real run produced

```text
documents      210
  from files     6
  from reviews 204
chunks         262
```

204 real customer reviews and 6 text files, all arriving in the same list. From
here nothing downstream knows or cares which is which, which is the point of
doing this step at all.

Those chunks are the input to 6.5, where they become searchable by meaning
rather than by word.

## Running it

**Free Databricks workspace:** open
`examples/03_ingest_unstructured/notebooks/run_document_index.py`. Three minutes
forty. The reviews are already in the workspace; the notebook creates the volume
and puts the six text files in it.

**On your own machine:** the same task, with the corpus pointed at a local
folder. One environment variable changes.

---

## What to take from this one

**A pile of files is a valid source.** You do not need to load them into a
database first.

**Cut with overlap.** The cost is storage and the saving is not being confidently
wrong later.

**Normalise early.** Two sources that arrive completely differently should stop
being different as soon as possible. After this task, one list.
