# Ubunye Engine Part 6.5: Asking a Question of Your Own Documents

*Example 05 of eleven. Runs in two minutes sixteen. Uses the chunks from 6.3.*

---

## The problem

Ask a language model about your company's refund policy and it will tell you,
fluently, in your own tone, and it will be making it up. It has never seen your
policy. It has seen thousands of other people's, and it is producing the average
of those with great confidence.

This is the single most common way organisations get burned by these tools. The
answer is not wrong in an obvious way. It is plausible, specific, and invented.

---

## What it looks like

---

## The idea, which is simpler than the name

The name for this is retrieval augmented generation, which tells you nothing.

Here is the whole idea: **do not ask the model what it knows. Find the relevant
paragraphs yourself, hand them over, and ask what they say.**

That changes the question from "what do you know about refunds", which invites
invention, to "according to these three paragraphs, what is the refund policy",
which is a reading comprehension task. Models are very good at reading
comprehension.

---

## Turning meaning into numbers

To find the relevant paragraphs you need to search by meaning rather than by
word. Somebody asking about "getting my money back" should find the paragraph
about refunds, even though it never uses that phrase.

The trick is to turn each chunk into a long list of numbers, arranged so that
**things that mean similar things end up near each other**. Refund and
reimbursement land close together. Refund and rainfall do not.

Then the question goes through the same process, and finding the relevant chunks
becomes measuring which ones are nearest. That is arithmetic, and computers are
fast at arithmetic.

This is task 1, and its output is a table of chunks with their numbers beside
them.

---

## Answering, and citing

Task 2 takes a question, turns it into numbers the same way, finds the nearest
chunks, and hands **only those** to the model along with the question.

It also keeps which chunks it used, and the answer comes back with its sources
attached. That matters more than it sounds: an answer you cannot check is an
answer you have to trust, and the whole point of this exercise was to stop
trusting.

On the real run it answered **five questions**, each with its sources and a
similarity score.

---

## The part worth testing: the refusal

There is one question in the example that the documents do not cover. Something
about swallows.

A system that invents an answer to that is dangerous, because it will invent an
answer to a question about your refund policy too, and you will not be able to
tell the difference.

So the example asks the unanswerable question on purpose, and checks that it
**says it does not know**. That refusal is asserted, not hoped for.

If you build one of these, make that your first test. Not whether it answers
well. Whether it declines when it should.

---

## Where the model comes from, which is business logic

On Databricks this uses the workspace's own hosted models. Off Databricks it
uses small open models that run on an ordinary processor with no account and no
key.

The interesting part is where that choice lives. Not in `config.yaml`, and not
in the engine. It is in a `serving.py` beside the task, because **which model
answers your customers is a business decision**, not a setting. One environment
variable switches it, and the pipeline does not move.

The answers from a small open model are worse than from a large hosted one, and
that is not the claim. The claim is that the process, chunk and embed and
retrieve and ground and cite, runs anywhere.

---

## Running it

**Free Databricks workspace:** run 6.3 first to produce the chunks, then the two
notebooks in `examples/05_rag_documents/notebooks/`. Two minutes sixteen.

**On your own machine:** set `MODEL_BACKEND=local` and it swaps the hosted models
for open ones that download once and then work offline. The build runs it this
way on every push, in a container with the weights baked into the image, because
a container that fetches a gigabyte on startup silently depends on the internet.

---

## What to take from this one

**Retrieve, then ask.** Never ask a model what it knows about your business.

**Cite the sources.** An answer nobody can check is not much better than a guess.

**Test the refusal first.** A system that never says "I do not know" is a system
that will invent something at the worst possible moment.
